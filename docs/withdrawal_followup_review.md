# Withdrawal evidence: review brief, 12 November 2026

The study can presently describe recorded civilian deaths around documented changes in MONUSCO presence. It cannot yet estimate how many deaths withdrawal caused. This follow-up corrects a timing interpretation and investigates a consequential geographic disagreement without altering the original outcomes.

## 1. Correct the 2017 timing interpretation

July 31, 2017 is the supported fixed-base closure anchor for Walikale-centre, Bunyapuri, Masisi-centre, Nyabiondo and Luofu. The [July announcement](https://www.unmissions.org/en/peacekeeping/news/closure-of-5-fixed-bases-in-the-north-kivu-province-for-the-purposes-of-more) gave the intended date; [Anadolu's August 1 report](https://aa.com.tr/fr/afrique/rdc-nord-kivu-quel-avenir-apr%C3%A8s-le-d%C3%A9part-de-la-monusco-/873948) described the five closures as having occurred on Monday, July 31. This supports a base-closure anchor, not a precise date when every convoy left or community protection ceased.

October 19 is the date of a retrospective Radio Okapi report in the existing source register. It is not evidence of an October departure. The old July-to-October envelope and October-anchor outputs are retained for reproducibility, but must not be interpreted as equally supported operational dates. The follow-up timing table labels them accordingly.

The [UN's August account](https://monusco.unmissions.org/fr/news/la-monusco-experimente-un-nouveau-dispositif-militaire-au-nord-kivu-apres-la-fermeture) describes mobile combat deployments from August 3 at Masisi-centre, Lukweti, Nyabiondo, Ntoto, Buleusa and Kabalo. These are named activities, not a comprehensive patrol log. Anadolu also records announced capacity to patrol from retained bases at Kanyabayonga and Kirumba; it does not demonstrate that particular patrols occurred. Therefore the exposure is fixed-base closure amid mobile reconfiguration, not complete withdrawal of UN protection.

Using the already-computed July anchor (24 baseline months; six follow-up months; transition month omitted), strict recorded civilian deaths are:

These are territorial descriptions, not base protection-area estimates. They do not identify effects or represent three independent withdrawal decisions.

## 2. Geographic disagreement remains consequential

The R audit examined all 85 unique records with a parsed administrative locality different from the point-based assignment in the saved eligible-event lineage. This expands beyond the 19 conflicts appearing in the displayed case windows. All five saved boundary layers were checked consistently. Agreement across maps does not provide five independent confirmations: boundary products can share source geometry.

For Luhanga, event 234588 (November 27, 2016), every layer assigns the stored coordinate to Walikale. Under SALB 2018 it is approximately 0.68 km from the polygon for the administratively reported Lubero. The older layers differ substantially in the location of that polygon. These distances diagnose map/coordinate inconsistency; they are not uncertainty radii.

The GED administrative label and [AFP/VOA](https://www.voaafrique.com/a/pres-de-34-civils-tues-dans-aatque-village-hutu-par-des-miliciens-nande-en-rdc/3613252.html) identify Lubero; [Radio Okapi](https://www.radiookapi.net/2016/11/29/actualite/securite/nord-kivu-inhumation-des-victimes-de-massacre-de-luhanga) also reports Lubero, with a later burial tally that differs from the provisional fatality report. Neither switching map products nor selecting a convenient death tally resolves this.

The separate reassignment scenario moves the stored 34 civilian deaths from Walikale to Lubero without changing the combined total. On the historical broad-envelope windows, Walikale's strict baseline falls from 43 to nine deaths, versus five in follow-up: the apparent monthly decline becomes an increase. This scenario uses the OLD envelope solely to isolate geographic assignment; use the July-anchor table above for the corrected closure description. No primary data were overwritten. The scenario is not a corrected dataset or causal estimate.

## 3. What still prevents a withdrawal-effect estimate

South Kivu's existing strict summary remains 71 recorded deaths over 24 baseline months and eight over six follow-up months. That decline is descriptive. Reporting completeness and protection catchments are not established. Public reporting does not tell us how many incidents went unrecorded. No new evidence in this follow-up resolves those gaps or establishes a credible comparison locality.

The next useful external records are dated patrol/activity summaries with locations and reporting-coverage records with explicit interruptions. Until they arrive, further effect regressions would add assumptions rather than resolve the design problem.
