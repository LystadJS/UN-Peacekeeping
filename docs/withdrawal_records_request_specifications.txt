MONUSCO withdrawal study: records request specifications
Prepared 11 November 2026. Status: prepared locally; not sent.

Purpose
Determine whether documented reductions in UN presence can be linked to changes
in recorded civilian deaths without confusing lost patrol coverage with a base
listing change, or lost reporting coverage with lower violence. This is an
academic feasibility inquiry, not a request for a predetermined finding.

REQUEST 1: HISTORICAL BASE OPERATIONS AND COMPLETED PATROLS

Initial routing: UN Archives and Records Management Section, arms@un.org.
Verified public routing source: https://archives.un.org/en/content/about-us
Ask which MONUSCO or DPO office holds the records if they are not in ARMS.
Do not assume that ARMS holds these records or can authorize their release.

Geography: Masisi-centre, Nyabiondo and Sake, North Kivu, including documented
communities visited from those bases. These are an interconnected operational
network, not presumed independent treated and untreated locations.
Retrieval dates: 1 July 2015 to 28 February 2018.

Stage 1: Does a releasable historical record series exist? Please identify its
custodian, date coverage, geographic detail, access conditions, missing periods,
and available dictionary or small example extract. Public redacted or aggregated
records are sufficient for this initial feasibility assessment.

Stage 2, only if feasible: Request an existing export or documents containing:
- Base opening, temporary suspension, closure, relocation and reopening dates;
  distinguish actual departure from announcement and formal handover.
- Completed patrol/mission date or interval; origin base; destination settlements
  or named areas; activity type; duration if recorded.
- Planned versus executed status and cancellations, if recorded.
- Whether an absent entry means no mission, incomplete reporting, or unknown.
- Reporting-unit changes and known gaps in the record series.
- Contemporary protection-priority areas or authorized area-of-responsibility
  descriptions, distinguishing plans from demonstrated reach.

Fallback: monthly completed-patrol totals by origin and destination, with coverage
flags; no personnel identities, beneficiary identities, current operational
routes or sensitive tactical detail are needed. If date precision is restricted,
retain the supplied intervals rather than inventing exact dates.

Anchor documents to help retrieval: OIOS audit 2017/011, the MONUSCO 11 August
2017 account of fixed-base closures and mobile deployments, and the UN briefing
covering 13-20 September 2017 describing the Sake-Nyabiondo mission of 15-16
September. These establish leads, not a complete patrol census.

REQUEST 2: HISTORICAL INCIDENT-MONITORING COVERAGE

Initial routing: SAR protection-monitoring team, contact@sarmp.unhcr.org.
Verified public routing source: https://sar-drc.unhcr.org/
Referral: DRC Protection Cluster information management team; current directory
lists Eric Bazie (information management) and Steve Ndikumwenayo (coordinator):
https://globalprotectioncluster.org/democratic-republic-congo
Mailbox delivery and continued staffing have not been tested.

Geography: South Kivu, specifically Kabare (including Bukavu), Kalehe, Fizi
(including Baraka), Idjwi, Mwenga, Shabunda, Uvira (including city) and Walungu.
Request explicit geographic identifiers and the distinction between cities and
territories. Priority areas surround Bunyakiri, Kavumu Airport
and Sange; do not assume a base location equals an entire territory's coverage.
Retrieval dates: 1 April 2022 to 30 November 2024.

Stage 1: Are historical monthly inputs to the monitoring-coverage dashboard or
partner coverage matrices retained and releasable? Request a dictionary, sample,
coverage dates, and an explanation of the public dashboard's reference period.
Clarify whether the linked August 2021 data page is obsolete and where archived
2022-2024 coverage records are held.

Stage 2, only if feasible: Request existing location-month metadata for:
- Organization and monitoring program, geographic unit and identifiers.
- Actual operating periods, active monitors or monitor-days where recorded.
- Full, partial, suspended, absent, or unknown monitoring coverage, using the
  provider's definitions rather than retrospectively invented categories.
- Access interruptions, logistical changes, staff redeployment and restart dates.
- Zero-incident reporting rules: whether zero reports were submitted, or whether
  silence is indistinguishable from no monitoring.
- Active surveillance versus passive receipt of reports; verification thresholds,
  changes in incident definitions, and geography changes (including whether
  South Kivu and Maniema were combined in published aggregates).
- Data version, retrospective additions and revised records where documented.

Fallback: dated coverage maps and interruption notes with the underlying
geographic legend. Partner service-delivery 3W/6W lists should be labelled as such;
they cannot substitute for incident-monitoring effort. No victim, witness or
monitor identities are requested. Incident-level personal data are unnecessary
for this first coverage inquiry.

Why this matters: the Protection Cluster 2024 annual report describes declining
atrocity-monitoring capacity after withdrawal; public reports still record
incidents afterward. We need to distinguish continuity of some reporting from
stable observation effort. Neither report totals nor staff totals alone establish
how many deaths were missed by UCDP or any other event dataset.

RECEIPT AND DECISION RULES

For each response record custodian, receipt date, version, permitted use,
geographic and temporal scope, missingness semantics, and completeness caveats.
Keep untouched source files and provenance before any R transformations.
Start with a sample: assess whether locations and dates can be matched under
the project's existing boundaries and fixed feasibility windows.
Do not change outcome definitions or treatment dates to obtain a better result.
Do not convert unknown coverage to zero patrols, zero monitors or zero deaths.
Do not use post-withdrawal monitoring effort as an ordinary regression control
and claim this recovers the total effect of withdrawal; it is an observation
process requiring separate measurement reasoning.
If only selected anecdotes or current snapshots exist, retain descriptive
inference. If records are unavailable, stop this acquisition route rather than
extend model search. Useful records clear measurement questions only; comparison
credibility, selection and spillovers still need separate adjudication.
